2. What the Program Includes
Ryp Academy runs from November through February. Through the academy, athletes and parents/guardians can use tokens for one-on-one mental performance sessions with Yannick Artigolle.
One token = one session of up to 30 minutes with either the athlete or a parent/guardian. Sessions are separate by default; a joint session with both in the same room or space is also possible (see Section 4). A joint session is a single session and uses one token. A joint session counts as one of the athlete's sessions toward the monthly limit. Session time includes any time spent arriving or settling in. Travel time to an offsite room (see Section 5) is not included.
Tokens are subject to Ryp Academy's token terms, including cost and expiration. The program ends at the end of February.
Athlete sessions come first. Parent/guardian sessions are available only alongside athlete sessions. For any athlete, the number of parent/guardian sessions may not exceed the number of athlete sessions. The parent(s)/guardian(s) decide how tokens are used, and the athlete will be told in advance whenever a parent/guardian or joint session is booked.
3. Understanding the Service: Mental Performance Coaching
Mental performance coaching helps athletes develop the mental skills needed to perform at their best. Sessions are designed to be a positive, supportive, and educational experience.
Focus Areas Include: Goal setting, building confidence, managing pressure and anxiety, improving focus and concentration, developing pre-shot routines, and bouncing back from setbacks.
What This Is NOT: This service is not therapy, psychological counseling, family counseling, parenting counseling, or medical treatment. Yannick Artigolle is a Mental Performance Coach, not a licensed psychologist or therapist. He does not diagnose or treat mental health disorders (e.g., clinical depression, anxiety disorders).
Referrals: If signs appear that fall outside performance coaching (such as an eating disorder, depression, anxiety beyond performance nerves, substance use, or self-harm), Yannick will tell the parent/guardian (or, for athletes 18 and older, encourage the athlete directly) and recommend a licensed professional from Ryp's referral list. He will let Ryp leadership know that a referral was made, without sharing details.
4. Parent/Guardian and Joint Sessions
Parent/guardian sessions exist to help you support your athlete's mental game at home, at practice, and at tournaments. They are not a space for your own counseling or for working through family or relationship concerns.
Athlete sessions and parent/guardian sessions are held separately by default. This protects the athlete's ability to speak openly.
Joint sessions. A joint session is a session with the athlete and a parent/guardian in the same room or space. Joint sessions are held only when both the athlete and the parent/guardian agree, and either of them may decline or ask to stop at any time. Yannick may also decline a joint session if he believes it is not serving the athlete.
Joint sessions are not family counseling. A joint session is a performance coaching conversation, not family counseling or therapy. Yannick's training is in mental performance coaching. If the conversation moves toward family conflict, relationship concerns, or mental health concerns, he will redirect it and may recommend a licensed professional (see Section 3).
Confidentiality in joint sessions. Everything said in a joint session is heard by everyone present and is not confidential among them. What the athlete shared in individual sessions stays confidential, and Yannick will not bring it up in a joint session unless the athlete has agreed to share it.
What you share in a parent/guardian session may inform the coach's work with your athlete. It is not confidential from your athlete. Please share only what you are comfortable having used in that way.
The athlete will know when a parent/guardian session is taking place.
Parent/guardian sessions and joint sessions are never audio recorded.
Parent/guardian and joint sessions can take place in any of the locations described in Section 5.
5. Session Locations & Privacy
Sessions can take place in one of three ways. Every session is scheduled through Ryp, so the time and location are on record. The parent(s)/guardian(s) choose, and the choice can be changed for any session by letting Yannick know in advance.
Option A: Academy Quiet Area (6529 Cecilia Cir, Edina, MN 55439). A designated quiet area at the academy, used during academy operating hours with another Ryp adult on site. It is in view of the training floor and within the facility's camera coverage (the cameras do not record audio). The coach will position the session out of earshot of others, but this is an open space shared with practicing athletes, coaches, and parents, with music and ball-striking noise. Other people may see the session, and privacy and confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.
Option B: Eden Prairie Conference Room (7500 Golden Triangle Dr, Eden Prairie, MN 55344). A windowed conference room at the Ryp office, used during the building's normal business hours while other occupants are present. The windows stay unobstructed and the door is closed but not locked. The room is well away from the waiting room near the entrance, so conversations cannot be heard from the waiting room, but because the room is windowed the session can be seen by people nearby. The location is approximately an 8-10 minute drive from the academy. For athletes under 18, the parent/guardian is responsible for getting the athlete to and from the room and knows the time and location. The parent/guardian may wait on site, ideally in the waiting room so you can leave together when the session ends. If the building is otherwise empty (for example, evenings or weekends), an athlete under 18 must have a parent/guardian on site for the whole session. Athletes 18 and older do not need a parent/guardian on site. Athletes 16 or older who drive may drive themselves only if a parent/guardian gives permission below and other occupants are present in the building. Yannick will not transport athletes.
Option C: Online (video). Online sessions use a Ryp-account video platform, with video on for both the coach and the athlete throughout. The coach joins from a professional setting at the academy, with his screen facing away from other people and headphones in to protect confidentiality. There may still be background noise, and privacy cannot be completely guaranteed on the coach's end. The athlete joins from a private, quiet room, and the family is responsible for making sure no one else is within earshot (other than a parent/guardian in a joint session). For athletes under 18, a parent/guardian must be at home during the session. While online platforms use encryption, absolute digital confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

6. Confidentiality Agreement
Building trust is essential for effective coaching. To create a safe space for the athlete to share openly, athlete sessions are confidential, with the limits described below.
For the Athlete: What you share with your coach, Yannick, will be kept private. He will not share the specific details of your conversations with your parents, teachers, or other coaches without your permission. In a joint session, your parent/guardian hears whatever is said in that session.
For the Parent/Guardian (athletes under 18): By default, you will receive your athlete's attendance and their general goals (for example, "we're working on confidence and pre-shot routines"), along with strategies you can use to support them. If you prefer not to receive these updates, tell Yannick; that is completely fine. You may decline or resume updates at any time. Specific, personal details from athlete sessions remain confidential to protect your athlete's trust in the process.
Athlete-Approved Sharing: Your athlete may choose to allow the coach to share more detail with you. This is always their choice, may be withdrawn at any time, and they will never be pressured to share. Golf and fitness coaches receive only what the athlete and parent/guardian agree to share.
Confidential, Not Secret: Confidentiality is not secrecy. Yannick never asks an athlete to keep anything from their parents, and the athlete decides what to share, subject to the safety exceptions below.
Parent and Joint Sessions: As described in Section 4, what you share in a parent/guardian session may be used in the coach's work with your athlete. Joint sessions follow the ground rules in Section 4.
Location: Confidentiality depends on where the session takes place. See Section 5, Options A, B, and C.
Limits to Confidentiality: Confidentiality does not apply where there is a risk of harm. The coach is required, by law or professional ethics, to break confidentiality and contact parents/guardians and/or the appropriate authorities if the athlete discloses:
Intent to harm themselves.
Intent to harm someone else.
Information about ongoing or past abuse or neglect of themselves or another minor.
Any information the coach is required to disclose by a court of law.
In these situations the coach will contact the parent/guardian the same day (for athletes 18 and older, the emergency contact the athlete lists), make any required report to child protection or other authorities, and notify Ryp leadership. For any other urgent concern about the athlete's immediate well-being (e.g., unexpected illness or distress during a session), the coach reserves the right to contact the parent(s)/guardian(s) or the Emergency Contact listed in Section 1.
7. Scheduling & Cancellations
Sessions are scheduled by appointment through Ryp channels that parents/guardians can see. Please provide at least 24 hours' notice to cancel or reschedule.
One grace session: The first late cancellation (less than 24 hours' notice) or no-show during the program will be rescheduled at no penalty, and the token will not be used.
After that, late cancellations and no-shows forfeit the token.
Tokens are subject to the expiration terms in Section 2.
8. Communication, Audio Recording & Client Records
Communication. For athletes under 18, Yannick communicates with parents/guardians and does not text athletes one-on-one. Session content is never discussed by text or social media. For brief questions or scheduling matters, you may contact Yannick at yannick@rypgolf.com. Extensive coaching discussions or confidential matters should be reserved for scheduled sessions.
Audio recording. Yannick may audio record one-on-one sessions with the athlete (never parent/guardian sessions or joint sessions). Recording happens only with written consent: parent/guardian consent and athlete assent for athletes under 18, or the athlete's own consent for athletes 18 and older. You may decline, or opt out at any time, including mid-program, with no effect on participation. Recordings are made on Yannick's personal phone and moved the same day into Ryp's Google Drive (a Ryp-owned system), after which they are deleted from the phone. They are stored only in Ryp-owned systems, are shared only with the athlete's consent, and are kept no longer than 30 days after the session unless the athlete or family asks for longer.
Client records. Session notes and any recordings are stored in a secure, access-restricted Ryp-owned system. Only Yannick has routine access. Ryp can access them only through its designated records custodians (Ryp's Founder & President and its CEO), with notice to Yannick unless the law prohibits it and with every access logged by individual user, and only to meet a legal obligation (such as a subpoena or court order), to recover records after the coach's death, incapacity, or catastrophic loss of the primary records, or to investigate a specific complaint or safety concern, in which case only the relevant records are opened. The access log is reviewed periodically by someone other than the custodians. Session notes are kept for 7 years after the last session, or until the athlete turns 25, whichever is later and then securely destroyed, unless a legal hold applies. If Yannick leaves Ryp, records stay in the Ryp system and are transferred to a successor coach only with the athlete's consent (the parent's, for athletes under 18); otherwise they are kept sealed for the rest of the retention period.
